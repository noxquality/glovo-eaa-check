Glovo, measured against its own statement

Glovo did something very few companies do: it published an accessibility statement and committed in writing to WCAG 2.1 Level AA and EN 301 549. That is the standard accessibility is measured against in Europe, so that is the standard used here.

The five specific promises in that document were checked against the product, one by one. Two hold, one holds halfway, and two do not. The two that fail are fixable, and the fix is smaller than it looks.

Independent, unsolicited measurement, 8 October 2026. Not affiliated with Glovo and not approved by Glovo. It measures the public product, the way anyone finds it without an account. It does not measure teams, it does not measure people, and no failure is attributed to anyone.

This piece makes no legal claim. The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025 and Spain transposed it through Ley 11/2023, but whether a company meets a legal requirement is for a regulator to decide, not for a private audit. What is checked here is narrower: whether the product does what the document the company published says it does.

Why

Measured against the statement, not against an outside opinion

Because Glovo picked the standard, not an auditor from outside.

Taking the document a company published in its own footer, and checking criterion by criterion whether the product does what it says, is harder to argue with than a list of things someone thinks could be better.

Promise 3 of 5 · halfway

"Error notifications: Clear and descriptive error messages to guide users in resolving issues."Glovo accessibility statement

One warning is announced and the other is not

The missing-address warning is textbook: role="alert", aria-live="assertive", and the text says what to do, not only what happened.

The required-options error is not. Adding a product without picking a required option paints a badge that reads "Required" and nothing else. No role="alert", no aria-live, no focus move. With a screen reader nothing is announced, so the user is left waiting for a button that already responded.

WCAG 3.3.1 Error Identification and 4.1.3 Status Messages, both Level AA.

Promise 4 of 5 · does not hold

"Keyboard accessibility: Functions that can be operated via keyboard."Glovo accessibility statement

The product's first step needs a mouse

The address field on the home page cannot be operated with a keyboard. It is the first thing the site asks for, before a single store is visible, and getting past it takes a mouse.

There is an alternative keyboard path, which is why this is a serious failure and not a full block. The path the product offers first, and the only one on screen, does not work.

WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard, Level A. Inside the scope the statement defines for itself.

Promise 5 of 5 · does not hold

"Accessible labels for interactive elements to ensure screen readers announce their function."Glovo accessibility statement

40 controls with no name, in the dialog every order goes through

The options that customise a product are 40 inputs with no accessible name, sitting inside rows marked role="button". 40 out of 40.

A screen reader announces controls without saying what they choose. This is the dialog for the base, the size and the extras, so any order with options passes through it.

WCAG 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value and 1.3.1 Info and Relationships.

The channel for reporting the problem

"If you encounter any difficulty accessing content or features, please reach out to our support team."Glovo accessibility statement, Limitations and Alternatives

Measured on 8 October, with no account, on the Barcelona home page: the "Contact us" link in the footer opens nothing. The URL changes and the page stays where it was.

Search answers anything with the same catalogue

Searching "motosierra", Spanish for chainsaw, returns 69% of the same stores as searching "pizza".

68 of 99 unique stores overlap. The first result for both queries is the same one, Domino's Pizza. And 41 of the 100 results for "motosierra" have "pizz" in their name.

The empty state is not missing: it exists and it works. What fails is the relevance floor, set low enough that a dictionary word the catalogue does not cover pulls 100 irrelevant stores instead of the empty state.

What it costs
With a keyboard you do not get past the first field, which is the address. With a screen reader the order stalls in the product dialog, where 40 controls go unannounced and so does the error. And the channel for reporting it does not open without an account.
The fix
Make the address trigger a real button that takes focus. One label per input in the product dialog, no visual redesign needed. role="alert" on the required-options error, plus a focus move. A skip link. Raise the relevance floor in search and use the empty state that already exists. Point the "Contact us" anchor at something.

Method and limits

What was measured. Desktop web, Barcelona, Carrer de Balmes 100, no login, cookies declined. Four pages: city home, category listing, search results and store page, plus the address dialog and the product dialog.

How. Direct URL navigation, inspection of the DOM and the accessibility tree, a keyboard pass counted Tab by Tab, and contrast calculated on computed colour and background.

Which version of the statement. The Spanish one, and that is worth saying because the two versions do not match. The Spanish text states that the covered parts "cumplen" (comply with) WCAG 2.1 AA and EN 301 549; the English text says they "observe" them. This piece measures against the Spanish version, which is the one a user in Spain is served.

When. There were two passes, one on 26 August 2026 and one on 8 October. The numbers here are from 8 October. Comparing the two is what makes it possible to say what got fixed in between.
